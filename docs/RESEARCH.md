# Research, attribution, and limitations

The guide’s research snapshot is dated **9 November 2026**. It is a targeted synthesis of institutional research, official guidance, employer pages, and learning resources. It is not an exhaustive review or a representative survey of jobs or investments.

The project was developed with AI assistance. Sources, arithmetic, and assumptions should remain inspectable; AI assistance is not a substitute for verification or a reason to claim expert validation.

## Distinguish the kind of evidence

| Evidence | What it can support | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A disclosed input and transparent calculation | The arithmetic consequences of those inputs | Whether the assumptions are realistic |
| A job advertisement | Requirements and compensation stated by that employer at that time | Actual vacancy, payroll, market-average wages, or an applicant’s hiring probability |
| An observational study | A measured association in a defined population | Causation or automatic transfer to another country or person |
| A projection | A scenario or forecast under stated assumptions | A guaranteed outcome or an investment return |
| Official rules | A starting point for eligibility or compliance | That rules have not changed, or that every individual qualifies |
| A recommendation in this guide | A reasoned option to test | A proven best career or a personalized financial forecast |

The local hiring example contains six postings from five employers. Two postings are from the same employer. It is a convenience sample, not six independent measurements of an entire labour market. Recheck dates, eligibility, hours, pay composition, and vacancy status directly.

## Add or update a source

1. Prefer the original research, official agency, employer, or documentation.
2. Record the title, publisher, URL, publication date when available, and date accessed.
3. Identify the exact claim supported by the source.
4. Describe geography, population, study design, and limitations that materially affect interpretation.
5. Preserve historical dates when discussing old findings. Mark a newer finding as an update, not as evidence that was known earlier.
6. Keep summaries brief and original. Link to the work; do not reproduce substantial copyrighted content.
7. Update the claim and its limitations together. A link replacement alone may not make old prose current.

For legal, tax, banking, visa, and financial-product information, prioritize official sources and make the need for current local verification clear. Do not invent a filing exemption, guaranteed yield, or entitlement.

## Change a recommendation

State the problem, alternatives, evidence, assumptions, downside, and a small useful test. Include material counterevidence. Explain what new facts would change the recommendation. Do not turn a desire for certainty into a score or success probability that the evidence cannot justify.

A source count is a description of the bibliography, not a quality score. More links are not necessarily better evidence.
