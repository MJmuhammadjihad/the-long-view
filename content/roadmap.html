04 / 24-week decision path

A plan that changes when the facts do.

Choose a concrete income target without spending as if it is already secured. Follow one primary earning route and make each review depend on observable progress.

Use a calendar to review progress, not certify ability.

Count week one from your actual start date; a four-to-six-month window is a planning horizon, not a promised time to employment. The gates below decide the next action. Missing a gate means change the scope, support or route—not pretend the calendar made you ready.

◷
Decision tool / capacity

Make the week add up

Set a sustainable ceiling for these commitments; the example is not a prescription. Count class, study, job, travel and care before assigning additional learning. Sleep and basic personal time must already be protected outside this ceiling.

The remainder is available for deliberate practice and buffer. It is not all guaranteed focused work. A full-time role may require fewer courses or a smaller side project.

The 24-week decision path

01
Weeks 1–2

Compare and choose

Evidence to inspect: Complete the route experiment, household cash facts and degree gate checks. Ask for external review of at least one work sample.

IF THE EVIDENCE IS THERE

Choose A, B or C as primary; retain one fallback.

IF IT IS NOT

If cash is already short, prioritize feasible income and a smaller learning block. Do not wait for a perfect career answer.

02
Weeks 3–4

Create a small proof

Evidence to inspect: A: a tested data tool. B: accurate bug reports and support walkthrough. C: a truthful sample of work you can sell or be hired to do. Another person can assess it.

IF THE EVIDENCE IS THERE

Begin relevant applications and bounded outreach while continuing practice.

IF IT IS NOT

If you cannot explain or change the work, narrow it and ask a knowledgeable reviewer to identify the specific gap.

03
Weeks 5–8

Demonstrate responsibility

Evidence to inspect: A: a small usable app with basic safety checks. B: test and diagnose an unfamiliar issue, explain a resolution and escalate correctly. C: perform the agreed task reliably.

IF THE EVIDENCE IS THERE

Apply to roles whose core requirements you can substantiate; seek supervision.

IF IT IS NOT

If output quality remains weak, favour a supervised route. If applications get no screens, review eligibility and presentation before changing your entire career.

04
Weeks 9–12

Compare progress with cash

Evidence to inspect: Review actual cash, bills, interviews, offers and collected payments. Separate unqualified interest from a signed agreement and from cash received.

IF THE EVIDENCE IS THERE

Continue where both ability and market response are improving within the funded runway.

IF IT IS NOT

If runway is shrinking and no credible offer or client is developing, activate bridge income, reduce costs and renegotiate education pacing now.

05
Weeks 13–16

Deliver and collect

Evidence to inspect: If hired, meet a narrow set of responsibilities and get feedback. If contracted, pass the acceptance test and collect payment. Record support and unpaid hours.

IF THE EVIDENCE IS THERE

Build reliability and evidence; adjust workload to the actual role.

IF IT IS NOT

A lower-paid first role can be rational if net cash and learning improve. Reject exploitative or financially unworkable terms, not simply every offer below the aspiration.

06
Weeks 17–24

Decide the next quarter

Evidence to inspect: Review three months of collected net income, real skill growth, references, academic progress and family obligations.

IF THE EVIDENCE IS THERE

Deepen a working route; raise responsibility or prices only when evidence supports it.

IF IT IS NOT

If below target, revise the next 90 days with a feasible income floor. Do not borrow for speculation or buy a guaranteed-success course.

Application or conversation counts are diagnostic prompts, not statistical proof that a career is dead. Log the quality and type of response. Weekly review: one capability gained, one market fact learned, cash collected, bills due and one change for next week.

The longer horizon remains conditional

Months 7–18

Establish a base

Increase responsibility, finish or resume a viable degree plan, and build reserves. Do not assume the first employer is your permanent ceiling.

Years 2–3

Specialize from experience

Choose a domain where you have evidence of demand and growing competence. Explore relocation using net income and verified eligibility.

Years 3–10

Accumulate ownership

Invest surplus and consider a repeatable business. Revisit the thesis annually; the exact technologies and opportunities will change.
