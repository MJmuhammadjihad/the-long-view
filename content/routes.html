01 / Choose your route

Choose a route with evidence.

You need a practical way into paid work. The market does not owe you an income because you chose a difficult skill or worked long hours.

The decision is still open.

Development can be a reasonable hypothesis for readers with relevant study or experience. It is not established as everyone’s fastest income route. Compare it with work that uses testing, support, implementation, communication or existing subject knowledge.

A 14-day reality test

Days 1–2

Measure constraints

Record available cash, confirmed support, bill dates, degree options and actual weekly commitments. Confirm how long any household support can continue; current support is not a six-month funding commitment.

Days 3–5

Try representative work

Spend bounded sessions on a CSV processing task, a QA/support task and one plausible bridge task. Use documentation; record where you need direct help. These are work samples, not validated aptitude tests.

Days 6–10

Gather market feedback

Extend the sample to about 20–30 nonduplicate relevant roles. Record student eligibility, hours, location, skills and disclosed pay. Seek feedback from two practitioners and contact reachable employers where qualified.

Days 11–14

Make one bounded commitment

Choose one primary route and one income fallback based on progress, eligibility, access and affordability. Commit for six to eight weeks, with a review date. Do not launch five parallel curricula.

How to evaluate your work fairly

Ask a reviewer to assess correctness, explanation, response to changed requirements, reliability and communication. A polished AI-assisted demo is weak evidence if you cannot diagnose its failures. A slow but correct improvement is useful evidence. Professionals use tools; independence means you can take responsibility for the result, not memorize everything.

A concrete day-14 decision

  • Choose A if you can show improving independent technical work and find attainable development roles or supervision.
  • Choose B if troubleshooting, test design and communication are stronger, with reachable support or QA opportunities.
  • Prioritize C if essential cash is becoming urgent or the technical gap needs longer. Keep a smaller learning block if feasible.
  • If evidence is mixed: choose the smallest next experiment with a real reviewer or employer. Do not turn two weeks into a permanent verdict on your ability.

What would change the recommendation

A concrete paid apprenticeship in a different stack outweighs my generic stack preference. Consistently stronger noncoding work can justify a different primary route. A short funding runway can outweigh long-term upside. Serious health, care or study constraints can make a slower schedule rational.

Do not choose by which field sounds most prestigious. Choose a feasible next step that improves cash flow, capability or access without creating an unaffordable downside.
