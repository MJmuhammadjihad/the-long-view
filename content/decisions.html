You will miss opportunities. You will also make some choices that turn out badly. The goal is to protect against ruin, learn quickly and make fewer avoidable mistakes.

“No bias” is not a promise anyone can honestly make. The useful standard is to expose assumptions, seek the strongest opposing case and say what evidence would change the recommendation.

What would change this recommendation?

A strong paid apprenticeship in another field, unaffordable degree bills, substantially better performance in support than development, an urgent family cash shock, or credible repeated demand for a service you can already deliver. Record those changes here. Do not stay loyal to a plan just because it is beautifully presented.

A small decision policy

Before a big commitment

Write down the decision, alternatives, costs, evidence and what would make you change your mind. Ask someone informed to argue the strongest case against it. Check the incentives of anyone selling you the opportunity.

Prefer small, informative experiments

Use a short project, a supervised role or a customer interview to learn. The best early experiment improves your skills even if the original business idea fails.

Review on a schedule

Review execution weekly, the earning strategy every month, and major career/degree choices after meaningful new evidence. Avoid changing direction each time a new technology trends.

Define enough

Write down the life you want to support: family needs, health, meaningful work, time and reasonable comfort. An endless comparison with the richest visible person makes every decision feel inadequate.

A useful test for any new opportunity

  1. Can I name a customer or employer that currently pays for this?
  2. Can I acquire enough skill to become useful within my available runway?
  3. Does the work create transferable capability, even if this market disappoints?
  4. Can I explain why someone would choose me and why that advantage might persist?
  5. Can I test the idea without endangering essential family money?
  6. What evidence would tell me to stop, simplify or choose another route?

These are decision prompts, not a numerical scoring formula. A fatal legal, safety or affordability problem cannot be averaged away by attractive points elsewhere.

The family-provider conversation

Agree with your household on what support is available for the next six months, what expenses you should take over first, and what would trigger a change. Do not promise to replace the entire family income by a date before knowing the numbers. A gradual transition, with shared visibility and a reserve, is more resilient.

Protect health, sleep and relationships as productive assets. A career that only works at an unsustainable pace is a fragile financial plan. A lower-status first job can still be a rational bridge toward better work.
